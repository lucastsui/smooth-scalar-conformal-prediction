At the final endpoint, use the value at the last knot. The map ψ is infinitely differentiable with every derivative zero at zero
from the right. Therefore h has zero derivatives of every positive order at both endpoints; adjacent interpolation pieces
and the constant exterior extensions have matching derivatives of all orders. Since their unscaled values lie in [0, 5/4] and
aK ≤ 1/8, scores lie in [0, 5/32] ⊂ [0, 1].

B. Additional Details for the Consistency Result
We make the compactness step explicit. If ω(w) ̸→ 0, choose η > 0, wj ↓ 0, parameters λj and intervals (tj , tj + wj ] with
probability at least η. Since scores lie in [0, 1], only interval locations in a bounded region can have positive probability for
small wj . Pass to a subsequence with λj → λ∗ and tj → t∗ . On the interval event,
                                 |Sλ∗ (Z) − t∗ | ≤ L∥λj − λ∗ ∥ + wj + |tj − t∗ | =: ej → 0.                                 (22)
For any fixed e > 0, all sufficiently large j imply P(|Sλ∗ (Z) − t∗ | ≤ e) ≥ η. Letting e ↓ 0 gives an atom of mass at least η,
a contradiction.
Thus for any r > 0, a sufficiently small parameter radius gives a rank net with error at most r. Its cardinality is finite and
does not depend on n. For every center, the beta mean is cn → 1 − α and its variance is k(n + 1 − k)/[(n + 1)2 (n + 2)] → 0.
A union bound over this fixed finite set gives convergence of both its maximum and minimum order statistics. This justifies
the bracket argument without requiring a rate for the concentration modulus. It also shows why allowing K and therefore
the score family to change with n falls outside the corollary.

Direct classical reduction under weaker conditions. Let Λ ⊂ Rd be compact, the scores bounded in [0, 1] and jointly
measurable in (z, λ), and suppose that at every fixed λ, Sµ (Z) → Sλ (Z) almost surely as µ → λ. Assume Fλ is continuous
for every λ. On the compact index set Λ × [−1, 2], consider a(z, (λ, t)) = 1{Sλ (z) ≤ t}. At each fixed (λ, t) it is almost
surely continuous in its index: the only possible obstruction is Sλ (Z) = t, which has probability zero. Its envelope is one.
Lemma 2.4 of Newey & McFadden (1994) therefore implies

in outer probability. At the selected kth order statistic, Fn,λ̂ (q) ≥ k/n and Fn,λ̂ (q − ) ≤ (k − 1)/n. Continuity of the
