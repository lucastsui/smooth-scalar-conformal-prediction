## Searches intended to falsify novelty

Queries included exact-title + counterexample/correction/erratum; scalar parameter + VC dimension; conformal + smooth/Lipschitz + tuning; conformal + anti-concentration; score selection + correlation; probability integral transform + covering; risk control + non-monotonicity. Following citations exposed the original CRC counterexample and the two 2026 follow-ups. Current arXiv versions changed the titles of the Yang/Kuchibhotla, Liang/Zhu/Barber and Aldirawi papers; the bibliography uses the verified titles of the cited versions.

The official Zeng implementation and its README were inspected for intended empirical scope. It uses ImageNet and CUDA-based model experiments; we did not reproduce those experiments and make no claim about their correctness. The OpenReview discussion was blocked by a browser verification page; it was not treated as read. No public correction was found in the accessible sources searched, which is not evidence that none exists. Citation-forward coverage remains incomplete.

## Established / proposed / unresolved

Established: fixed-score conformal validity; beta order statistics; model-selection optimism; no general identification of parameter count with VC dimension; non-monotone CRC failures; covering arguments with CDF regularity.

Proved in the draft: explicit strengthened construction and occupancy identity; rank-cover lower bound for its knot subclass; rank-net beta specialization; the compact-family corollary is explicitly attributed to classical uniform convergence. The proofs, not simulations, carry the universal claims.

Unresolved: whether these conformal-specific refinements are sufficiently novel and significant for a full ICML paper; whether an overlooked theorem subsumes the exact construction; practical utility of a rank-cover certificate without population information; behavior of ordinary trained-network tuning procedures. An external domain expert should assess these before submission.

## Revision: classical uniform convergence

Reviewer A identified a direct prior result in [Newey and McFadden (1994), Lemma 2.4](https://www.ssc.wisc.edu/~xshi/econ715/chap36neweymacfadden.pdf), printed p.2129. The root researcher independently read this lemma and its qualifications. Applied to score sublevel indicators, it establishes the qualitative consistency claim under weaker pointwise almost-sure continuity. The revised manuscript credits this result and includes the reduction; consistency is removed as a standalone novelty claim. The rank-cover finite-sample specialization and exact smooth occupancy construction remain the proposed contribution. This narrows significance rather than improving the novelty assessment by assertion.
