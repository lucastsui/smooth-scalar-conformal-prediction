**Reviewer A v1: novelty and significance**

This is a separate AI review, not external expert validation or an official ICML review. I assessed the frozen manuscript independently of the other review passes. Date: September 28, 2026. The reviewed PDF's SHA-256 is e8e9d171e386a08b1027bb598fae41c183976c4db9027e6fb85f0da90d264229; all four frozen-file hashes match their manifest.

**Neutral summary.** The paper studies ordinary split-conformal calibration when the same sample selects the score. Its main negative example embeds all subsets of a finite label universe into a compact, smooth, bounded, uniformly Lipschitz scalar score family. Selecting the observed label set gives exact marginal coverage \(c_n[1-(1-1/K)^n]\), despite atomless score distributions. It then gives a union-bound certificate based on sup-norm covers of population-transformed scores, relates those covers to raw-score regularity and anti-concentration, and proves consistency for each fixed compact family. Synthetic experiments check the formulas and compare oracle lower certificates. The intended contribution is a correction and clarification of assumptions, not a deployable calibration method.

**Strongest merits.**

1. **The correction targets a real published claim.** I inspected Zeng et al.'s published Proposition 4.6, Lemmas D.3/D.5, and the relevant proof text. They do infer VC bounds from parameter dimension and bounded continuity. The manuscript's finite-label shattering construction directly contradicts those assumptions, including after constant extension outside the compact interval. This is a legitimate and potentially useful correction. It is also appropriately separated from a criticism of the original empirical measurements. [Zeng et al., published paper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/zeng25e/zeng25e.pdf)

2. **The strengthened example is clean and unusually explicit.** The distinction between raw-score smoothness and statistical complexity is transparent: positive rescaling controls derivatives without changing prediction sets. The independent uniform component removes ties without obscuring the failure. The exact occupancy calculation makes both catastrophic finite-sample failure across families and recovery within a fixed family easy to verify. I did not find a prior source presenting this exact combination and formula.

3. **The manuscript is candid about scope.** It prominently discloses exponential distinguishability, the mask implementation for large \(K\), the combinatorial selector, the oracle nature of the certificate, and the absence of practical temperature-scaling evidence. The results and controls support the stated implementation-check purpose. These qualifications prevent several otherwise serious overclaims.

**Strongest reasons for rejection in its present form.**

1. **The fixed-family consistency corollary is already a consequence of a classical, more general theorem.** This is the most concrete missing positioning I found. Newey and McFadden (1994), Lemma 2.4, printed p. 2129, gives uniform convergence for a compact parameter set, a function continuous at each fixed parameter almost surely, and an integrable envelope. Apply it to
   \[
   a(z,(\lambda,t))=\mathbf 1\{S_\lambda(z)\le t\},\qquad
   (\lambda,t)\in\Lambda\times[-1,2].
   \]
   Parameter continuity of \(S_\lambda(z)\), together with \(P\{S_\lambda(Z)=t\}=0\), supplies the required almost-sure continuity at each fixed pair; the envelope is one. Thus
   \[
   \Delta_n=\sup_{\lambda,t}|\widehat F_\lambda(t)-F_\lambda(t)|\to0.
   \]
   For any selected \(k\)-th calibration order statistic, the empirical CDF and its left limit give
   \[
   k/n-\Delta_n\le F_{\hat\lambda}(q_{\hat\lambda})
       \le (k-1)/n+\Delta_n.
   \]
   This proves the manuscript's conclusion, even without uniform Lipschitzness. The source states the result in outer probability when necessary; a measurable selected coverage is sufficient for the implication used here. This reduction is my inference from the inspected theorem, not a claim that the chapter printed this conformal corollary. The manuscript acknowledges classical ingredients, but it should explicitly position Corollary 4 as a standard uniform-convergence consequence rather than rely on it as an independent novelty pillar. [Newey and McFadden, Section 2.3](https://www.ssc.wisc.edu/~xshi/econ715/chap36neweymacfadden.pdf)

2. **The negative result's remaining novelty is narrow relative to the familiar mechanism.** Angelopoulos et al.'s Proposition 1 and its proof already give arbitrarily bad scalar non-monotone selection using many alternatives on a compact interval. Aldirawi et al.'s Section 3.1 analyzes the corresponding finite-grid failure as sample size and grid resolution vary. Neither inspected construction is identical to this manuscript's smooth, atomless, recalibrated-score construction, so I do not regard them as an exact subsumption. Nevertheless, the manuscript's statistical mechanism is memorization of observed labels; its formula is the observed-support probability multiplied by an independent conformal order-statistic mean. Smooth interpolation and a positive scale factor package a finite class into one coordinate. Proposition 3's packing bound then witnesses the deliberately encoded exponential class. This is an elegant strengthening and correction, but I am not yet convinced it changes what an informed reader should believe about adaptive calibration beyond making a known warning more explicit. [Conformal Risk Control, Section 2.4 and Proposition 1 proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.02814), [Aldirawi et al. v2, Section 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01502v2)

3. **The positive certificate does not yet establish a sufficiently distinct consequence.** Theorem 2 follows from the classical fixed-score binomial/beta law, a union bound, and the deterministic Lipschitz property of order statistics. Vovk already supplies the exact fixed-score conditional-validity ingredient, and Yang and Kuchibhotla give finite-family reused-calibration conditional bounds. Covering arguments with score-CDF regularity also appear in Kiyani et al. The probability-scale formulation is sensible and rescaling invariant, but the current paper supplies neither a sharp lower bound showing when this complexity is necessary nor a useful class-specific repair of the challenged dimension claim. Its nontrivial numerical example uses known \(B=L=1\) and a raw-parameter net; at \(n=999,a=1\), the continuum lower certificate is 0.85755 versus 0.86333 for the finite-grid beta bound, and only 0.1% of repetitions fall below it. These facts do not disprove the theorem, but they provide limited evidence of an advantage attributable to the new formulation. A theory paper need not include a large benchmark; a sharper theoretical consequence could address this concern. [Vovk, Proposition 2b](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v25/vovk12/vovk12.pdf), [Yang and Kuchibhotla, Theorem 1 and proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.13871), [Kiyani et al., Assumption 2 and Theorem 4.4](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/b41907dd4df5c60f86216b73fe0c7465-Paper-Conference.pdf)

**Questions whose answers could change the assessment.**

- After recognizing the classical uniform-convergence reduction, what is the precise new theorem beyond the strengthened finite-class counterexample and the convenient certificate formulation?
- Can the analysis repair a dimension-based guarantee for a named, useful calibration family under verifiable structural assumptions, or prove a tight relationship between a suitable rank complexity and worst-case selection loss?
- Can one exhibit a nontrivial family for which probability-scale geometry yields a meaningfully better guarantee than existing empirical-process or finite-grid analysis? The current linear example does not isolate that benefit.
- What substantive obstacle prevents deriving the main strengthened counterexample by smoothing and rescaling a standard finite-class failure example? A precise comparison would help distinguish a useful correction from a full new theoretical contribution.

**Tentative recommendation and confidence.** Weak reject for a full ICML research paper in its present form; moderate confidence. The correction is real, and the exact construction may be original. My concern is the amount of new insight after separating out established selection failures and standard concentration results. I would consider the work valuable as a focused correction or research note. A consequential class-specific repair or a substantially sharper complexity result could change this assessment. I am not claiming an exact prior duplicate or predicting an actual program committee decision.
