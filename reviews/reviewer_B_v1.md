**Neutral summary**

The paper embeds all subsets of a finite label universe into a smooth, bounded, Lipschitz scalar score family. A selector memorizes the observed labels, and ordinary recalibration yields conditional coverage equal to the fraction of labels seen times a uniform order statistic. This gives an exact occupancy law and disproves a uniform sample-size guarantee based only on parameter dimension, a common raw-score Lipschitz upper bound, and a common score range. The positive results use sup-norm covers of population-transformed scores and beta order statistics. Compactness, uniform raw-score Lipschitzness, and atomlessness then imply consistency for each fixed family and distribution.

My assessment is that the central mathematical claims are correct under the stated assumptions. I found no fatal technical error. The reported numerical results reproduce, and independent calculations agree. The main reservations concern the limited new theory and practical reach, plus two minor implementation limitations outside the frozen experimental settings.

**Strongest merits**

- The construction separates smooth raw scores from the complexity of their threshold decisions. The exact occupancy identity makes the failure transparent, and the smooth interpolation does satisfy the promised common derivative and range bounds.
- The manuscript correctly distinguishes failure uniformly across changing families from consistency of one fixed family. This qualification is mathematically essential and is maintained in the text and figures.
- The rank-cover proof permits arbitrary measurable selectors, including selectors that create calibration ties. It does not assume independence between cover representatives or a beta law for the adaptively selected score.
- The finite precision limitation, the discontinuous selection loss, the oracle nature of the positive certificate, and the synthetic scope of the experiments are disclosed. The numerical uncertainty is calculated at the calibration-sample level.

**Proof audit: all substantive steps**

| Claim or step | Assessment and reasoning |
|---|---|
| Setup and fixed-score beta law, Section 3 | Correct for `1 <= k <= n` and continuous score CDFs. The probability integral transform is uniform even if the CDF has flat regions. Applying the CDF commutes with taking an order statistic. Independence of the new test observation justifies the conditional coverage formula. |
| Smooth interpolation, Section 4 and Appendix A | Correct. The denominator defining `h` never vanishes. Flatness at both endpoints lets every derivative match between intervals and constant extensions. On the left half, `g(t) <= 2 - 1/t`; writing `v = 1/t`, the proposed envelope is `exp(2-v)(v^2+4)`. Its derivative is negative for `v >= 2`, and its value at 2 is 8. Symmetry gives the other half. Multiplication by `a_K(M-1)=1/8` gives the asserted derivative upper bound. |
| Boundedness and atomlessness | Correct. Scores lie in `[0,5/32]`, hence `[0,1]`. At every fixed parameter the distribution is a mixture of finite-width uniform distributions. A density bound `4/a_K` is valid, including at interpolation parameters where intervals overlap. Its deterioration with `K` is the relevant missing uniform regularity. |
| Selection loss | Correct for the stated unrestricted selector. Adding an observed label reduces the exclusion loss by at least 1 and increases the size penalty by only `1/(K+1)`; adding an unobserved label only increases the penalty. Thus the selected subset is uniquely the set of observed labels. The finite-valued knot selector is measurable. Infinite loss away from knots is not a smooth optimization method, but the paper does not claim that it is. |
| Theorem 1, conditional coverage | Correct. All selected calibration scores are `a_K U_i/4`; a test label outside the selected subset has score at least `a_K`, above the threshold. An included label is covered exactly when its independent uniform feature is at most `U_(k)`. |
| Theorem 1, marginal coverage | Correct. The occupancy count depends only on labels and is independent of all calibration uniforms. Summing the probability that each label is seen gives `K[1-(1-1/K)^n]`. The uniform order-statistic mean is `k/(n+1)`. These facts justify multiplying the expectations. |
| Boundary conventions and uniform impossibility | `K=1` reduces to fixed-score coverage. `k=n+1` gives coverage one and is correctly excluded from the occupancy formula. For the finite-threshold case, the union bound `1-(1-1/K)^n <= n/K` proves the claimed arbitrarily small coverage at fixed `n`. Increasing `K` changes the family; the paper does not mistake this for fixed-family inconsistency. |
| VC shattering and cited claim | Correct. At the `K` points `(u=1/2,y)`, membership scores equal `a_K/8` and nonmembership scores equal `9a_K/8`. Threshold `a_K/2` therefore realizes each subset. The constant extension supplies a bounded continuous family on all of `R`, as required by the general inequality in Zeng et al.'s Lemma D.3. The published D.3/D.5 statements do assert the continuity-to-VC implication being challenged. I inspected the local primary PDF/text, pp. 19–20; the [official published paper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/zeng25e/zeng25e.pdf) is the relevant source. This does not refute results for separately restricted practical calibrator classes. |
| Population ranks and increasing transformations | Correct. A strictly increasing score transformation preserves every comparison, so its CDF evaluated at the transformed score equals the original population rank. The common-domain sup-norm qualification avoids changing functions on parameter-dependent null sets. |
| Theorem 2, deterministic bracket | Correct. The `k`th order statistic is 1-Lipschitz in the coordinatewise sup norm. Every selected rank vector is therefore within `r` of some representative's vector. This proves the minimum/maximum bracket pathwise, even if selection causes ties. No measurable nearest-center choice is needed for the bracket. |
| Theorem 2, tails and inversion | Correct. Each fixed representative has a beta-distributed order statistic. The lower event is contained in a union of representative lower events, and the upper event is contained in the corresponding upper union. Dependence between representatives is immaterial. The beta distribution is continuous, so the strict inequalities and inverse-CDF certificate are consistent. Clipping the lower certificate at zero is valid. |
| Proposition 3, rank packing | Correct. At an equal-size knot, the rank is `pu` for included labels and `p+(1-p)u` otherwise. Distinct subsets have a differing label and distance at least `p`; a ball of radius strictly below `p/2` cannot contain both. In fact, when `p <= 1/2`, endpoint calculations give separation `1-p`, so the stated lower bound is conservative rather than excessive. |
| Raw-score to rank transfer, Section 5.1 | Correct, including the factor 2. If the scores differ uniformly by at most `h`, then `{S_mu(Z) <= S_mu(z)-2h}` is contained in `{S_lambda(Z) <= S_lambda(z)}`, which is contained in `{S_mu(Z) <= S_mu(z)+2h}`. Each CDF increment has probability at most `omega(2h)`. A common density bound gives `2BL epsilon`. Common positive rescaling changes `B` and `L` inversely. |
| Corollary 4, uniform anti-concentration | Correct. If shrinking intervals retained positive mass, compactness gives a convergent parameter subsequence, and bounded scores give a convergent interval-location subsequence. Uniform Lipschitzness then contains the corresponding events in shrinking neighborhoods of one limiting score value. Continuity from above of probability would imply an atom. The argument does not silently require a common density upper bound. |
| Corollary 4, coverage and expectation limits | Correct. For any fixed rank tolerance, compactness and the preceding transfer give a finite cover independent of `n`. The representative beta means converge to `1-alpha` and variances to zero. A finite union bound controls both extrema in the bracket; sending the fixed tolerance to zero gives convergence in probability for every sequence of selectors. Coverage is bounded in `[0,1]`, so convergence of its expectation follows. |
| Linear-family density and CDF | Correct mathematically. Convolution with the independent uniform response gives density at most 1; `|X| <= 1` gives score Lipschitz constant 1. The four positive-part squares are the integrated clipped-affine CDF. The family is uniformly bounded for each fixed slope range; if applying the corollary literally, a common affine rescaling places it in `[0,1]`. |
| Finite-grid DKW and continuum certificates | Correct for the reported finite-threshold cases. A fixed candidate has empirical CDF value `k/n` at its calibration quantile almost surely. The two-sided DKW union bound yields the stated expression. `ceil(a/epsilon)+1` equally spaced points on `[-a,a]` have covering radius at most `epsilon`. Choosing the maximizing radius using analytical constants only is deterministic and needs no extra multiplicity correction. These are single-setting confidence statements, as disclosed. |

I specifically tried to invalidate the positive results through adaptive ties, flat score CDFs, dependence among representatives, nonattainment of a concentration-modulus supremum, and a sequence of distributions developing narrow peaks inside a fixed family. None defeats the stated proof. In the last case compactness and uniform score continuity force a limiting atom, which the assumptions exclude. The reference to “each fixed n” in Section 4's impossibility paragraph could be qualified with “with `k <= n`” for clarity; the surrounding theorem and appendix already impose that restriction.

**Concrete technical issues requiring minor revision**

1. **B1 — numerically unstable CDF near zero slope.** In `src/core.py:52–60`, subtracting nearly equal squares and dividing by a tiny nonzero slope causes cancellation. Executed examples: `linear_cdf(0.5, 1e-16)` returns approximately `0.5551115123`, and `linear_cdf(0.5, 1e-20)` returns `0.0`; the exact answer is `0.5` in both cases. A stable piecewise convolution formula, or a carefully justified small-slope branch, should replace the cancellation-prone evaluation if this is intended as reusable code. **Effect on the paper:** none found. For all 6,000 saved linear outcomes, an independently implemented piecewise integration differs by at most `9.55e-15`. The frozen grid's smallest nonzero absolute slope is `0.001953125`, far from these failures.

2. **B2 — incomplete infinite-threshold handling in the linear path.** `continuum_lower(3, .1, 1)` reports a lower bound of zero after processing an invalid beta shape, although the convention makes coverage identically one. Zero remains a valid but needlessly vacuous lower bound. More substantially, `experiments.linear` with `n=3, alpha=.1` raises `ValueError: kth(=3) out of bounds (3)` at `src/experiments.py:67`. Either explicitly restrict this routine to `k <= n`, or implement the same infinite-threshold convention already used in the occupancy and finite-beta routines. **Effect on the paper:** none; all six frozen linear settings have `k <= n`, and the theorem explicitly excludes the invalid beta case.

Evidence for both issues is in `B_checks/check_results.json` and `B_checks/linear_boundary_probe.json`. These are not counterexamples to the mathematical statements and are not sufficient reasons to reject the manuscript on correctness grounds.

**Reasons a full-paper rejection could still be justified**

I found no demonstrated correctness defect that warrants rejection. The strongest remaining grounds are contribution and scope, not mathematical invalidity:

- The positive theory is a short synthesis of a probability integral transform, order-statistic stability, a finite union bound, and compactness. Theorem 2's proof and Appendix B make this directly visible. The paper does not establish an optimal rate, a matching general necessity theorem, or a way to obtain the required population quantities from data. If the intended contribution is a substantial new guarantee, the present technical increment may be too small.
- The empirical evidence is almost entirely verification of proved identities. The severe settings evaluate the algebraically equivalent mask event, and the regular-family experiment selects from 257 fixed slopes. Neither evaluates a realistic smooth tuning procedure nor optimizes over a continuum. The paper accurately limits its claims, but these checks do not independently establish practical value or prevalence.
- The counterexample's unrestricted combinatorial selector and shrinking score scale make the general impossibility persuasive, while leaving a large gap to useful classes of low-dimensional tuning. A full ICML contribution may require a sharper characterization of that boundary, beyond the current sufficient oracle condition. Whether the explicit smooth atomless construction and exact occupancy law already supply enough novelty is outside what this technical audit establishes.

These are possible grounds for an overall rejection, not claims that every theoretical paper needs practical experiments or a new algorithm.

**Questions that could change the assessment**

- Can the authors demonstrate a nontrivial class for which the rank certificate is both sharper than existing class-complexity guarantees and calculable from independently justified information?
- Is there a sharp sample-complexity statement, necessity result, or materially tighter certificate connecting the packing lower bound to coverage loss, rather than only showing that the adverse example has a large cover?
- How much of the smooth-score construction and the compact-family consistency result is subsumed by existing general uniform-convergence results or the cited non-monotone risk-control counterexamples? A precise reduction or separation would settle the significance concern more directly.
- Will the released implementation either handle the two edge cases above or state and enforce the narrower numerical domain it supports?

**Unverified items and limits of this review**

This was not a proof-assistant verification, independent human review, exhaustive literature search, or a fresh random-seed replication of the entire suite. The full reproduction intentionally uses the same seeds; the additional direct and stress checks use a separate review seed. Finite checks cannot establish infinite differentiability or a universal covering theorem; those were assessed analytically. Huge-precision scalar parameters were not instantiated, nor were real neural-network calibrators, non-i.i.d. data, atomic score distributions, or estimated oracle constants evaluated. I verified the targeted Zeng lemmas' stated assumptions, not every claim in all cited papers. I did not read the other AI reviews before reaching this assessment.

**Recommendation and confidence**

**Weak accept on technical validity, with minor revisions (6/10); confidence 4/5 for the claims assessed.** The main theorems and numerical evidence withstand this audit. I would not recommend rejection on demonstrated correctness grounds. This recommendation does not establish that the contribution meets ICML's novelty and significance threshold; that remains a substantive open question requiring the other review dimensions and external expert judgment.
