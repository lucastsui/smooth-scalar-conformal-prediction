**Reviewer A v2: novelty and significance**

This is a separate AI review, not external expert validation or an official ICML review. Date: September 28, 2026. I reviewed the frozen revised manuscript with PDF SHA-256 af9c88d9acfcac97d3663b442a7abd358fb9fc4f2d6f20d9d5e3618fb78bf004. The first review is preserved.

**Neutral summary.** The revised paper gives a compact, smooth, bounded, 1-Lipschitz scalar family of atomless conformal scores for which selecting the observed label set and reusing its calibration sample produces coverage \(c_n[1-(1-1/K)^n]\). It connects this example to an exponential packing of population-transformed scores and a beta-tail covering certificate. Fixed-family consistency is now explicitly presented as a consequence of classical uniform convergence. The numerical work illustrates the construction and the conservatism of oracle certificates; the paper makes no claim about the prevalence of catastrophic failure in ordinary temperature scaling.

**Assessment of attribution and scope.** The major attribution concern from v1 is resolved. The abstract, contribution list, Related Work, Corollary 4's label, explanatory paragraph, Appendix B, and conclusion consistently identify consistency as classical. Appendix B applies Newey and McFadden's Lemma 2.4 to the sublevel-indicator class on the joint parameter/threshold index set, includes the outer-probability qualification, and uses both empirical-CDF limits at the selected order statistic. The displayed sandwich handles ties created by selection. Its weaker parameter-continuity condition accurately explains why a uniform Lipschitz assumption is unnecessary for this qualitative result. I checked this against the primary chapter, including its measurability footnote. I no longer count A1 as an unresolved rejection reason. [Newey and McFadden, Section 2.3, Lemma 2.4](https://www.ssc.wisc.edu/~xshi/econ715/chap36neweymacfadden.pdf)

One minor scope wording ambiguity remains: Section 7 says that “Ties” are outside the stated results, whereas Appendix B explicitly permits calibration ties induced by selection. Specifying atom-containing fixed-score distributions would make that limitation precise. This is not a new substantive objection to the attribution repair.

**Strongest merits.**

1. **The correction remains legitimate.** The published Zeng Lemmas D.3/D.5 infer VC-dimension bounds from bounded parameter continuity, and the draft's explicit shattering example violates that conclusion while satisfying stronger smoothness assumptions. The revised finite-\(n\) paragraph also states the required \(k\le n\) condition. A transparent counterexample to a published general guarantee can be valuable. [Zeng et al., Proposition 4.6 and Appendix D](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/zeng25e/zeng25e.pdf)

2. **The exact construction is informative within its scope.** Atomlessness, smooth raw scores, explicit coverage, and the fixed-family/changing-family distinction coexist in one readily checked example. The rescaling argument explains why a raw-score derivative bound by itself cannot control calibration reuse.

3. **The revised presentation separates established theory from the proposed contribution.** It does not convert the added classical citation or implementation fixes into a novelty claim. The issue-resolution record correctly distinguishes repaired attribution from unresolved significance. Increasing scalar precision, the large discrete class, the combinatorial selector, population information, and numerical conservatism remain visible.

**Strongest reasons for rejection in the present form.**

1. **Consequential originality of the counterexample remains unresolved.** Theorem 1, its subset selector, its occupancy calculation, and Proposition 3's packing argument are substantively unchanged. The broad mechanism is still selection from a deliberately large finite class, packaged into a scalar using smooth interpolation and rescaling. CRC Proposition 1 already establishes arbitrarily bad compact scalar non-monotone selection; Aldirawi et al. Section 3.1 studies its dependence on grid size and sample size. The present smooth, atomless, recalibrated-score construction is a more specific statement, and I have not identified an exact antecedent for that combination. Nevertheless, the paper does not yet establish why this strengthening provides enough additional understanding for a full ICML research contribution beyond a useful correction. Correcting attribution removes an objection; it does not enlarge that remaining contribution. [CRC, Section 2.4 and Proposition 1 proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.02814), [Aldirawi et al. v2, Section 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01502v2)

2. **The certificate remains a convenient synthesis without a demonstrated distinctive consequence.** Theorem 2 still combines a fixed-score beta law, the sup-norm stability of order statistics, and a union bound. Proposition 3 lower-bounds a cover of the constructed class; it does not establish that this cover is a necessary complexity measure for adaptive coverage generally, or give a matching coverage lower bound. Existing work supplies exact fixed-score conditional bounds and finite-family selection bounds. The linear example does not isolate an advantage of probability-scale geometry: it uses known \(B=L=1\), and its continuum certificate is sometimes weaker than the finite-grid beta bound. For \(n=999,a=1\), the frozen table gives 85.7552% versus 86.3330%, with only one of 1,000 repetitions below the continuum certificate. These are properly disclosed limitations rather than false claims. They leave the scientific significance concern in A3 open. A sharper characterization or a useful class-specific repair could resolve this without requiring a large practical benchmark. [Vovk, Proposition 2b](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v25/vovk12/vovk12.pdf), [Yang and Kuchibhotla, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.13871)

**Questions whose answers could change the assessment.**

- What consequential conclusion is available from the strengthened smooth/atomless construction that is unavailable from a standard finite-class selection failure, beyond disproving the published general dimension claim?
- Can the rank-based analysis yield a sharp relation between complexity and possible coverage loss, or a justified dimension/rate guarantee for a useful named calibration family?
- Can a nontrivial example demonstrate a meaningful benefit from population-rank geometry over an appropriately chosen existing empirical-process or finite-grid bound?

**Tentative recommendation and confidence.** Weak reject for a full ICML research paper; moderate confidence, unchanged. The attribution and scope are now substantially sound, and the correction may merit a focused research note. My remaining concern is the amount and consequence of the original result, not resistance to the revision or a requirement that theoretical work include a practical algorithm. I have not established an exact prior duplicate, and literature-search limitations remain.
